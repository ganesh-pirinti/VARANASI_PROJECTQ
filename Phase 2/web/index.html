🗼 Scenario: Interference-Aware Frequency Channel Planning
Mapping Real-World Wireless Co-Channel Isolation to Binary Network Partitioning
Official PS-O1 Instance

In dense cellular networks (such as 5G microcells and university campus layouts), adjacent cell towers transmitting on overlapping radio frequencies create mutual co-channel interference. This degrades the Signal-to-Interference-plus-Noise Ratio (SINR) and causes packet drops. Telecommunication engineers must partition base stations across available orthogonal frequency bands to isolate interference.

1. Physical Entities & Interference Graph

The 6 communication towers form vertices $V=\{0..5\}$. The 9 weighted links represent interference severity $w_{uv}$ incurred if both towers share a frequency band. Total interference weight = 27.

2. Two-Channel Max-Cut Formulation

We allocate Channel $A$ ($x_i=1$) and Channel $B$ ($x_i=0$). Maximizing cut weight isolates neighboring towers onto orthogonal frequencies, achieving 81.48% (22/27) weighted cut fraction on the synthetic benchmark.

3. Illustrative Mapping Scope & Disclosure

Scientific Disclosure: This scenario provides an illustrative domain interpretation for the locked synthetic PS-O1 instance. Commercial radio planning requires multi-channel coloring ($K > 2$), power control, and dynamic scheduling.
